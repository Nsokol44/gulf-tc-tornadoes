## Preliminary status

The SPC CSV carries no preliminary flag column. SPC labels daily current year reports preliminary, but this archive ends with the 2025 season and was posted in April 2026, after that season closed, so 2025 is treated as an archived season. TCTOR is a living database whose entries are open to evidence based revision (its README says so), and HURDAT2 best tracks are subject to later reanalysis, so recent season counts can change in future releases of either file.

## Timezone convention

SPC tornado times are recorded in Central Standard Time: the tz column carries code 3 (CST in the SPC database description) for 73,421 of 73,458 records and for every record in the 1995 to 2025 analysis window. The 37 records with other codes fall in 1956 to 1994, outside the window. Convention applied: SPC times are fixed CST (UTC-06:00, no daylight saving shift) and are converted to UTC by adding 6 hours before matching to HURDAT2 (UTC) and to TCTOR (whose workbook carries both UTC and CST fields, which agree with this conversion). If a local office in fact logged an event in daylight time, that event carries a one hour timing error into the interpolated centre position; TCTOR notes the same class of timing imprecision in the parent data.

## The recovered rules and the design they produced

Moore et al. (2017) state no numeric association rule. Their sample is the TCTOR list: manual, case by case verification of membership in the circulation envelope of a classified or remnant TC, with no fixed radius, no time cutoff and no landfall requirement, covering 1995 to 2015 in the version they used (1296 tornadoes, 1285 after removing 11 records with path width 0 m). Geometry in the 2017 paper is relative to true north: diagonal quadrants, cardinal quadrants offset by 45 degrees, and north/south plus east/west halves. Its distance binning is distance from the coastline (0-100, 100-200, 200-300, over 300 km), never annuli from the centre. Its headline intensity contrast is weak F/EF 0-1 versus strong F/EF 2-3 (94 versus 6 percent in their sample). The text phrase F/EF 1-2 in their Section 3.2.4 is a misprint for 2-3 (per the methods recovery, from their Table 11) and is not used.
Moore and Dixon (2011), recovered secondhand: tornado within 400 km of the TC centre, from the day before landfall through the day after landfall, Gulf Coast landfalling hurricanes only.
Design adopted here, in consequence: (a) TCTOR itself is the historical sample for its covered years, 1995 to 2023, restricted to qualifying Gulf TCs and with the 2017 zero width edit. (b) For 2024 to 2025, the only years TCTOR does not cover, attribution uses the broad operational rule (800 km, whole HURDAT2 track window, Gulf qualifying TCs of any classified stage), the rule that recovers 93.9 percent of Gulf TCTOR tornadoes on the overlap years. (c) A 500 km variant of the same rule and the much narrower Moore and Dixon (2011) rule (400 km, landfall day minus 1 to plus 1, Gulf hurricane landfalls only; 33.7 percent of TCTOR on the overlap years) are run over all years as sensitivities, and their agreement with TCTOR on the overlap years is reported in the tables and summary JSON.

Interpolation check against TCTOR worksheet distances: median absolute difference 0.386 km over 1529 compared rows.

## Operational definitions

Qualifying Gulf TC (used for the TCTOR restriction and the broad operational rule): a HURDAT2 storm with a landfall identifier L fix at longitude 98.5 W to 81.0 W and latitude 31.5 N or less, or with at least one fix inside the near coast Gulf box (latitude 24.0 to 31.5 N, longitude 98.5 to 81.0 W) while classified as a tropical or subtropical system. The candidate list is saved at data/qualifying_gulf_tcs.csv (180 storms for 1995 to 2025).
Moore and Dixon (2011) rule as implemented: first HURDAT2 fix with identifier L at hurricane status at a US Gulf Coast position (longitude 98.5 W to 81.0 W, latitude 24.0 to 31.5 N) defines the landfall. The latitude floor matters: Ida (2021) first entered the wider Gulf box with a Cuban landfall on 27 August, and anchoring on that fix would place the window two days before its Louisiana landfall and score it zero tornadoes. The window is the UTC calendar dates from one day before to one day after that date; centre interpolated linearly between surrounding fixes (all fixes, including the 3 hourly landfall fixes); distance 400 km or less; nearest TC wins ties.
Broad operational rule as implemented: tornado time between the first and last HURDAT2 fix of a qualifying Gulf TC, interpolated centre, distance 800 km or less (500 km variant saved separately). The 800 km radius is a stand-in for the TCTOR circulation envelope: Moore et al. (2017) Figure 3 notes only 5 of their 1285 tornadoes lay beyond 800 km from centre.
Distance from coastline: nearest distance from the tornado start point to the Natural Earth 1:10m coastline, computed in EPSG:5070 (CONUS Albers). This measures distance to any coastline in the clipped North American window, which for a small number of border tornadoes may be a Mexican or Cuban coastline segment rather than a US one.

## Deviations and known limits

1. Gulf restriction. Moore et al. (2017) used an all US TCTOR sample with no geographic filter. The primary sample here restricts TCTOR to qualifying Gulf TCs, following the tasking for this manuscript and the Gulf focus of Moore and Dixon (2011). Study era totals here are therefore Gulf subsets and do not equal the 1296 or 1285 of the 2017 paper.
2. Attribution footing across the extension boundary. Extension era rows for 2016 to 2023 are TCTOR attributions (manual, case by case verified), while 2024 to 2025 rows come from the broad operational rule (800 km, whole track window). The 2024 to 2025 counts are therefore rule based rather than verified, and the remaining break in the record is verified versus operational attribution, not a change of radius: the broad rule is the same rule as the homogeneous operational series in Section 9 of the tables, provided for comparisons that need one rule throughout. The named storm table shows the narrower Moore and Dixon (2011) rule alongside, so the size of the rule dependence is visible (for example Helene 2024: 31 tornadoes under the broad rule, 16 under MD2011).
3. Remnant tornadoes. TCTOR includes remnant cases with subjectively analysed centres after HURDAT classification ends. Those rows are in the primary sample with TCTOR geometry, but no operational rule here can reproduce them, which is part of why the operational rules match only part of TCTOR (Section 9 of the tables).
4. TCTOR geometry is taken as published in the workbook (its bearing and distance columns), rather than recomputed, for TCTOR rows. The recomputation check above shows the two agree to a median of 0.386 km, so this choice does not move any binned result materially.
5. Zero width edit applied to TCTOR rows only, as in 2017. The 2024 to 2025 operational rows keep all records (none had zero width in those years in the SPC file attributed set: see the analysis log), and width statistics use recorded widths as they stand.
6. The MD2011 window is implemented in UTC calendar days, because the recovered rule is stated in days. A CST calendar day convention would shift the window by 6 hours at each end and could move a small number of edge cases.
7. F/EF pooling across the 2007 transition follows the 2017 paper's stated assumption. It is not tested here.
8. Reporting change across 1995 to 2025 (radar, spotting, cameras) inflates recent weak tornado counts independently of any climate signal. Era comparisons are descriptive and are not adjusted for it.
9. Individual distances and bearings inherit the 0.1 degree rounding of HURDAT2 fixes and SPC timing imprecision (TCTOR notes 64 percent of 1995 to 2008 record times end in 0 or 5 minutes). Read them as TC scale tendencies, not precise per tornado values.
