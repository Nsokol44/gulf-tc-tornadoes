# TC tornado climatology tables

Primary sample: the SPC TCTOR attributed list for qualifying Gulf Coast TCs, 1995 to 2023 (with the zero width edit of Moore, Sokol and Blume 2017), plus the broad operational rule (800 km, full track window) for 2024 to 2025, the years the current TCTOR release does not cover. Study era is 1995 to 2015. Extension era is 2016 to 2025. Operational sensitivity rules are reported in Section 9. Full provenance, the recovered rules and every deviation are in climatology_provenance.md.

## 4. Intensity: weak versus strong (the 2017 headline split)

Moore et al. (2017) summarise intensity as weak F/EF 0-1 versus strong F/EF 2-3, with a full sample split of 94 percent weak and 6 percent strong in their 1285 tornado sample. Shares here are percentages of tornadoes with a known rating. No tornado in the primary sample is rated 4 or 5 in either era.
| Group | N | Unknown rating | Weak 0-1 | Weak share (%) | Strong 2-3 | Strong share (%) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| TC primary, study 1995-2015 | 1200 | 0 | 1123 | 93.58 | 77 | 6.42 |
| TC primary, extension 2016-2025 | 545 | 17 | 488 | 92.42 | 40 | 7.58 |
| TC primary, pooled | 1745 | 17 | 1611 | 93.23 | 117 | 6.77 |
| All US, study 1995-2015 | 26080 | 21 | 23220 | 89.11 | 2687 | 10.31 |
| All US, extension 2016-2025 | 13184 | 1525 | 10243 | 87.85 | 1384 | 11.87 |

Full rating distribution and EF2+ / EF3+ shares (percentages of known ratings):
| Group | F/EF0 | F/EF1 | F/EF2 | F/EF3 | F/EF4 | F/EF5 | EF2+ share (%) | EF3+ share (%) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| TC primary, study | 774 | 349 | 74 | 3 | 0 | 0 | 6.42 | 0.25 |
| TC primary, extension | 285 | 203 | 33 | 7 | 0 | 0 | 7.58 | 1.33 |
| All US, study | 16016 | 7204 | 2085 | 602 | 138 | 14 | 10.89 | 2.89 |
| All US, extension | 5337 | 4906 | 1136 | 248 | 31 | 1 | 12.15 | 2.4 |

The F scale applies before 1 February 2007 and the EF scale from that date. Following Moore et al. (2017), ratings are pooled across the transition as a single F/EF variable, which is an assumption of that paper, not a tested result: the scales were designed to be consistent, but the damage indicator set and survey practice changed in 2007, and the study era straddles the break. Unknown ratings total 17 in the extension era and none in the study era: 9 TCTOR rows for 2019 to 2022 carry a blank rating in the workbook, and 8 of the 2024 to 2025 SPC records carry the SPC unknown rating code -9. They are excluded from the shares. For the all US comparison series, note that SPC unknown ratings become common in recent years (1,525 of 13,184 extension era records, against 21 of 26,080 in the study era), so the all US extension era shares rest on the 88 percent of records with a known rating and could shift if the unrated records, mostly tornadoes with no damage survey, were rated.

## 5. Size (path length, path width, DPI)
| Era | Length median (km) | Length Q1 | Length Q3 | Length mean (km) | Width median (m) | Width Q1 | Width Q3 | Width mean (m) | DPI median |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Study 1995-2015 | 1.61 | 0.483 | 4.641 | 3.592 | 45.7 | 27.42 | 73.12 | 71.837 | 0.028 |
| Extension 2016-2025 | 2.543 | 0.837 | 6.939 | 5.746 | 68.58 | 45.7 | 137.16 | 126.384 | 0.123 |

Caveats: path width is the maximum observed width, recorded as such since 1994, so both eras use maximum width. Width and length are still survey based damage measures: survey practice, aerial survey use and the population of damage indicators changed across 1995 to 2025, and widths where damage indicators are sparse are prone to understatement, so era differences mix real change with reporting change. DPI is path area times (rating + 1); for TCTOR rows the TCTOR value is used, for 2024 to 2025 rows it is computed from SPC length, width and rating.

## 6. Geometry within the TC environment (true north)

All sectors are relative to true north, the convention of Moore et al. (2017). Diagonal quadrants: NE bearing 0 to 90 degrees, SE 90 to 180, SW 180 to 270, NW 270 to 360. Cardinal quadrants are the same scheme offset by 45 degrees. No motion relative sectors are used in the 2017 paper; none are used here.
| Era | NE | SE | SW | NW |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Study | 785 (65.42%) | 349 (29.08%) | 7 (0.58%) | 59 (4.92%) |
| Extension | 405 (74.31%) | 129 (23.67%) | 3 (0.55%) | 8 (1.47%) |
| Pooled | 1190 (68.19%) | 478 (27.39%) | 10 (0.57%) | 67 (3.84%) |

Cardinal quadrants, pooled: NoC 384 (22.01%), EoC 1235 (70.77%), SoC 115 (6.59%), WoC 11 (0.63%).
Halves, pooled: north half 1257 (72.03%), south half 488 (27.97%), east half 1668 (95.59%), west half 77 (4.41%).
Single storm leverage check: south of centre holds 115 pooled tornadoes, of which Rita (2005) produced 38 (33.04 percent). Moore et al. (2017) flagged the same pattern in their sample (Rita 39 of 100 south of centre tornadoes). Era totals are also concentrated: the largest single storm contributes 9.83 percent of the study era total (IVAN 2004) and 12.48 percent of the extension era total (BERYL 2024). Era comparisons should be read with that concentration in mind.

## 7. Distance from the coastline (the 2017 distance bins)

Distance is measured from the tornado start point to the nearest coastline (Natural Earth 1:10m) and binned as in Moore et al. (2017). The paper does not bin tornadoes by distance from the TC centre; centre range annuli are therefore not a headline output here. Pooled centre distances for reference: median 316.486 km, interquartile range 206.761 to 413.711 km.
